## Required Reading and Sources
To make it easier to start my research, I have structured my reading by effort: *low effort*, *medium effort* and *High Effort*: 

  1. *Low Effort*: Short reading times of 15 to 30 minutes, aimed to give a basic understanding of the topic I'm researching. Videos from youtube are also included in this category: 
    - [Wikipedia: Reaction–diffusion system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction%E2%80%93diffusion_system) and [Turing pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_pattern): overview of the model and its biological motivation
    - [Coding Train: Coding Challenge #13, Reaction Diffusion Algorithm](https://thecodingtrain.com/challenges/13-reaction-diffusion) ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BV9ny785UNc)): live implementation of the Gray-Scott model in p5.js
    - [Karl Sims: Reaction-Diffusion Tutorial](https://karlsims.com/rd.html): intuitive explanation of feed/kill rates, the discrete Laplacian, and the (k, f) parameter map

  2. *Medium Effort*: Semi-long reading times of 30 Minutes to 45 Minutes, aimed to give a more technical understanding of topic with definitions and theorems related to the topic. Reading matirail include longer videos, tutorials for math concepts from textbooks / internet, etc.   
    - [Robert Munafo: Reaction-Diffusion by the Gray-Scott Model, Pearson's Parametrization](https://www.mrob.com/pub/comp/xmorphia/index.html): term-by-term breakdown of the PDEs and a classified map of pattern types across parameter space
    - [Red Blob Games: Turing Patterns notes](https://redblobgames.com/x/2202-turing-patterns): worked exploration of Gray-Scott, including parameter sensitivity and convention differences between sources
    - [Numerical MOOC: Reaction-Diffusion notebook](https://nbviewer.jupyter.org/github/numerical-mooc/numerical-mooc/blob/remove_pyc/lessons/04_spreadout/04_06_Reaction_Diffusion.ipynb): forward-time/central-space discretisation of Gray-Scott in Python
    - Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos* (2nd ed.), Ch. 5–6 (linear systems, phase plane) and Ch. 8 (bifurcations in 2D): the linearisation and trace–determinant tools needed for Turing stability analysis
    - [Kondo & Miura (2010), Reaction-diffusion model as a framework for understanding biological pattern formation](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1179047), *Science*: accessible review connecting the theory to real animal patterns

  3. *High Effort*: Long reading times of up to 60 Minutes. The reading material is much more complex and targeted, requires a lot effort to understand. Material includes Research papers, advanced math topics and full lectures:
     - [Turing (1952), The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1952.0012), *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*: the original diffusion-driven instability argument
    - [Pearson (1993), Complex Patterns in a Simple System](https://arxiv.org/abs/patt-sol/9304003), *Science* 261: the Gray-Scott parameter classification used by most graphics implementations
    - [Turk (1991), Generating Textures on Arbitrary Surfaces Using Reaction-Diffusion](https://doi.org/10.1145/122718.122749), SIGGRAPH '91: reaction-diffusion applied directly to 3D surfaces
    - [Witkin & Kass (1991), Reaction-Diffusion Textures](https://doi.org/10.1145/122718.122750), SIGGRAPH '91: anisotropic and spatially varying reaction-diffusion for texture synthesis
    - Murray, *Mathematical Biology II: Spatial Models and Biomedical Applications* (3rd ed.), Ch. 2: full derivation of the Turing instability conditions and the critical wavenumber, which is the core of the linear stability analysis
